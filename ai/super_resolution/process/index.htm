The process

  1. Run the source image/video and driving image/video through a modified version of LivePortrait which exports a low resolution image, transformation data and st-maps.
  2. Warp the original high resolution with an upscaled version of the st-map. This is a 64x64x3 to 4096x4096x2 scale change. Notice the areas of occlusion where the mouth opens.
    Determine significant medium and low frequency changes between the low resolution and high resolution warped images. This will find occlusion and significant shadow movements.
    Using the mask, make a composite of the high resolution image's upper middle + high frequencies. The low resolution areas come through with the difference mask and globally in the low frequencies to keep the colour and luminance consistant.
  3. Cut the eyes and mouth out of the composite for an inpainting reference. The occlusions come through and the high resolution texture comes in where it can. These will steer the ControlNet.
    We can also take an eye from the original image for a texture guide (via an IP-Adapter) for the inpainting. We can't do this for the mouth due to heavy occlusion.


  4. Take the inpainted results and position them so they will go between the low resolution image and the high resolution image with the mask applied to the high resolution image as before.


  5. Apply the base transformations. Note the output has improved since this image was taking by improving the 64x64 to 4096x4096 st_map upscaling. This has made the eyes more accurate and removed innacurate waves in the hair. It is expected that further improvements can be made by improving the masking calculations to help the inpainting and transistions. Click on any photo to see a larger version.
